ENTRY 01 28 AUGUST 2026 SINGLE

NEW RELEASE

Attention ft Zeefu

A vibrant collision of desire, confidence and magnetic energy. Owirri and Zeefu step into a vivid sonic exchange made to be felt loudly.

“Attention” captures the moment subtlety leaves the room. It is direct, colourful and fully aware of its own pull—a song about wanting presence that can be felt, not merely promised.

The release opens another door into Owirri’s expanding sound: playful, expressive and unafraid to take up space.

ENTRY 02 9 MARCH 2026 EP

BEHIND THE PROJECT

BLACK

A cushion to bounce back on when that life cycle repeats itself.

“BLACK” lives inside recurring emotional cycles: the feelings we recognise, the patterns we revisit and the strength required to return to ourselves again.

Across three songs, the project makes space for difficult emotions without allowing them to become the final destination. It is vulnerable, reflective and built for the bounce-back.

ENTRY 03 2025 EP

A LOVE STUDY

What Love Looks Like

Six songs looking at love not as one perfect answer, but as something lived, questioned and constantly redefined.

“What Love Looks Like” explores the shapes love takes through desire, uncertainty, compromise, tenderness and self-discovery. Each song holds a different piece of the question.

On 20 September 2025, the project became the centre of Owirri’s first listening party—a room where private songs finally became a shared experience.

ENTRY 04 NOVEMBER 2023 THE BEGINNING

FROM STAGE TO STREAMING

Muziki Kama Hii

The first public creation. The first official release. The song that made a career feel possible.

In October 2023, Owirri entered Boomplay Campus MVP at Multimedia University of Kenya. Unable to settle on a cover song, she wrote an original instead and performed it publicly for the first time.

She finished fourth, but audience members left asking where they could find her music. The honest answer was nowhere—so one month later, “Muziki Kama Hii” became her first official release.

That one question—“Where can we listen?”—turned a campus performance into the beginning of an artist career.
