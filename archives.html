ENTRY 049 MARCH 2026EP DIARY

DEEPER THAN THE SKIN ON MY BACK

BLACK

A record of what it feels like to be young, ambitious and searching for oxygen in a world that sometimes forgets to breathe with you.

Behind the EP

Ever hit rock bottom so hard you start wondering if there's another floor beneath it?

That was me sometime last year.

Unemployment had landed on my shoulders like a stubborn storm. One of those seasons where you stare at your degree and wonder if all those late-night study sessions were just an elaborate prank life played on you.

At the same time, I was holding onto a version of love that felt more like survival than affection. I thought maybe it would give me something to hold onto. Instead, it taught me something even more important: sometimes the only person meant to carry you through the dark is you.

What I didn't realise in that moment was that my music was quietly growing.

While everything else in my life felt like it was collapsing, creativity was building pressure beneath the surface. And pressure, when you handle it right, makes diamonds.

Every morning I would wake up and spend the first hour staring at the ceiling, doing mental arithmetic with my life: unemployed, broke, ambitious and somehow still hopeful. It was a strange contradiction. Young, educated, talented... yet feeling invisible inside a system that didn't seem to have a seat reserved for me.

So I did what artists have always done throughout history.

Job applications by day, music composition by night, and recording whenever I could.

Outside my window, the country was vibrating with tension. Maandamano in the streets. Political debates in matatus. Conversations about taxes, corruption and survival. It felt like everyone around me was carrying something heavy inside their chest.

Escaping would have been easier. But instead, I turned the pressure into sound.

That's how BLACK was born.

Not from luxury. Not from comfort. But from exhaustion, reflection and the stubborn belief that something meaningful can grow out of difficult seasons.

BLACK is not just a musical project; it is a timestamp.

Why name the EP BLACK?

For a while, I debated naming the EP 254Flow, but then I realised something: 254Flow is just one expression of the larger story. Calling the entire project that would limit it to a location, when the truth is much bigger.

BLACK, to me, is not sadness. BLACK is depth. It is the colour of the soil we come from. The colour of resilience. The colour that absorbs everything and still holds its shape. BLACK represents the silent understanding between people who have faced pressure and decided to rise anyway.

Black on black, paint my dreams on that.

Even when life feels dark, it is still a canvas.

The moment Kenya is living in

Kenya is slowly moving toward another election cycle in 2027. Anyone who has lived here long enough knows the emotional weight political seasons carry: the frustration, the hope, the anger and the endless debates.

This EP isn't propaganda. It's perspective. It's what life feels like for a young Kenyan trying to build a future in a country that is still figuring itself out.

Track by track

254FLOW

This is the voice of the streets. I wrote this song right after leaving campus, navigating job applications, interviews and the quiet panic that arrives when the world suddenly expects you to have everything figured out.

254Flow is not just about unemployment. When I featured Brima Maovete, he brought in the layer that mentions patriotic revolution, overtaxation, bad and dormant leaders, amassed budgets, national debts and injustice, just to mention a few.

The hook, “254 yeah I still got the flow”, is both confidence and protest. Because no matter how heavy things get, Kenyan creativity refuses to die.

We still speak.
We still rap.
We still create.

BLACK

If this EP had a spine, this would be it. This song explores identity and resistance.

Being a Black woman in today's world means carrying both softness and armour. Sometimes you have to wear a gangster mask just to protect your peace. But beneath that armour, the spirit is still there. Still dreaming. Still creating. Still refusing to shrink.

Tears of Gold

This is the sound of the grind. Everyone celebrates success, but very few talk about the pressure that comes before it: the sleepless nights, the sacrifices and the moments when you question whether you're crazy for believing in your dreams.

“Tears of Gold” is a reminder that struggle is not wasted energy. Sometimes pain is simply raw material waiting to be refined.

Talk

The quietest song on the project and maybe the most necessary.

In a society where everyone is carrying something heavy, silence can become dangerous. “Talk” is about vulnerability. About creating spaces where people can finally exhale. Because sometimes healing begins with something simple: a conversation.

The visual language of BLACK

The cover art shows me dressed in black, standing against the Kenyan flag. That contrast is intentional. The flag represents the country where these struggles live. The black clothing represents the strength required to move through them. This project is deeply Kenyan and BLACK.

But the emotions inside it belong to anyone who has ever had to fight quietly for their future.

What I hope you feel

If you listen to BLACK during a difficult season, I hope you feel less alone.

If you listen while building your dreams, I hope it fuels your courage.

If you listen while feeling frustrated with the system, I hope the music helps you transform that anger into power.

Music cannot fix a country. But it can give people the energy to keep going and sometimes, that's enough.

A note from me

This EP became something unexpected. A cushion I can fall on when life gets heavy again. Because cycles repeat. Struggle returns. But when we turn those experiences into art, into music, into community, even our hardest seasons begin to mean something.

And eventually... tears turn to gold.

- Owirri

If BLACK resonates with your story, share it with someone who might need it too. Because in the end, we're all documenting this era together.

ENTRY 0330 NOVEMBER 2025EP DIARY

LOVE AT ITS FULLEST BLOOM

What Love
Looks Like Part I

The romantic experience of an African woman navigating love in a modern world.

About What Love Looks Like

What Love Looks Like reflects my personal journey of self-discovery, cultural roots and identity. I created it in a season of self-discovery, a process that left me raw but also gave me healing, as I uncovered more of who I am and what love means to me.

My cultural roots are woven delicately into this project, shaping its sound and spirit. Through these songs, I hope to affirm that our experiences are valid, beautiful and worth celebrating, even in their complexities.

The message I want to share is one of authenticity: to embrace our ways of life, our unique perceptions of beauty and the ways we show love in African spaces. Because love is rarely expressed fully in words alone, it calls for intention, presence and truth.

Part I captures the sweetness, tension and joy of love in its fullest bloom, told through the lens of a young African creative embracing culture, romance and soft rebellion.

Love is universal but personal. Everyone's version of love is valid, and there is no one-box definition of what love should look like.

By being raw, vulnerable and authentic in my music, I hope others feel less alone.

Tracklist

  1. Body Language
  2. Compromise ft Myq Twst
  3. Sinner ft Cece the G.O.A.T
  4. Last Night ft Izzy
  5. Skin ft Myq Twst
  6. Do Anything

This EP is only one side of the coin of love. Brought to life by my designated producer, Wanglo, it is written entirely by me apart from the few instances of collaboration.

As I feature love from the perspective of appeal, each song acts as a chapter in this story: some rooted in intimacy, others in vulnerability, compromise or seduction. The sonic palette spans Afro-fusion, soul-pop and experimental R&B, all anchored in authentic storytelling.

A glimpse into the sound

1. Body Language

An energy-packed dancehall track that flips the script on the typical party scene. Instead of chasing the dancefloor, it celebrates the sweeter decision: to stay in, share time and let love set the rhythm.

Built on infectious riddims and sensual grooves, the song captures the heat of romance: unspoken gestures, laughter and the slow sway of bodies moving in sync. It's about the kind of love that doesn't need words, only presence.

I wrote “Body Language” to showcase what love really feels like: not flashy, not performative, but deeply felt. It's romantic, playful and alive with the pulse of dancehall, reminding us that sometimes the real party is in the connection between two souls.

2. Compromise ft Myq Twst

Amapiano grooves laced with Luo flavour: smooth, danceable and proudly rooted.

Ka en ka mae ema hera chalo to an adwaro!
If this is what love looks like, then I want it!

3. Sinner ft Cece the G.O.A.T

Afro-R&B with a rebellious twist: soft edges wrapped in a rogue, gangsta glow. Gangster loving!

4. Last Night ft Izzy

Afro-pop with a sultry, club-ready energy: the soundtrack to late-night sparks. Fell in love from the nightlife!

5. Skin ft Myq Twst

Chill Afro-soul meets Luo poetry: tender, laid-back and deeply intimate. Where comfort lies.

“For love is the greatest commandment. You will find love everywhere... You will find love anywhere.”- Myq Twst

Myq shared that the lyrics and vibration, fusing urban sound and Luo identity, connected directly with his people and inspired his contribution.

“I caught on, then played some solo on the beat and a massive creation was built. This is and should be a hit song. Big up to Owirri for creating something as beautiful.”- Sunrise Alfie

6. Do Anything

An Afro-house anthem with Luo undertones: uplifting, bold and made for movement.

Kila ng'ato ni gi mare, pile pile in to ema mara!
Everyone has their own; every day, you are mine.

The listening party: love, loud and in the room

The listening party was never about perfection or polish. It was about presence. About sharing sound in the same room and letting it breathe between us. The performances were raw and alive. Moments of stillness met moments of loud joy. “Skin” felt especially tender in the room; you could feel people listening with their whole bodies. The energy was earned.

We gathered, greeted and laughed. Some people I knew well; others I was meeting for the first time. All were bound by curiosity and love for music. We captured the night in pictures, shared smiles and raised spirits. Those images hold more than memories; they hold proof that this music found its people.

What stayed with me most was the exchange. I didn't just perform; I received. Stories, affirmations, encouragement and reflection. The listening party reminded me why I create: to heal in public, connect without performance and let love show up exactly as it is.

That night was just the beginning.

ENTRY 0228 MARCH 2025ORIGIN STORY

THE SONG THAT FOUND MY PURPOSE

Lost & Found
in Rhythm

The making of Muziki Kama Hii: my first public creation, first official release and the moment music became a career.

Sometime in October 2023, I was knee-deep in my final year of campus, staring at the finish line with a mix of excitement and low-key panic. The future felt like an unsolved puzzle - fresh graduates in Kenya know the drill. Opportunities were scarce, and even though I was pursuing a course I'd been passionate about for a whole decade, that didn't make the transition from student to working adult any easier.

I was at a crossroads, battling self-identity struggles while trying to sneak a peek into my own future. What's next after graduation? With all these thoughts clouding my mind, I needed something to ground me.

Then, out of nowhere, The African Campus Battle popped up: a music competition that felt like a divine distraction sent from heaven. Music has always been my escape. Growing up in church, singing was second nature to me. So I figured, why not throw myself into something I love and let it carry me through the storm?

But then came the real struggle: what to perform? I've got a million and one favourite songs, and picking just one felt impossible. So I decided to do it my own way. I grabbed a pen and started jotting down thoughts, not overthinking, just writing about things I love and what I wanted to do. No grand vision, just pure expression.

That's how Muziki Kama Hii was born ... well, the first rough draft of it.

My first performance didn't exactly play out like the masterpiece in my head. I had some hiccups with my guitarist, the song structure was a little jumbled and the message wasn't quite hitting the spots I was aiming for. But instead of letting that shake me, I kept pushing.

Shortly after, another opportunity knocked: The Campus MVP Tour by Boomplay. This was my chance to reclaim my moment, and best believe I wasn't letting it slip.

This time, I did things differently. I put in the hours, refined my song and got a playback track made instead of relying on a guitarist. When I hit that stage, something clicked.

Even though I didn't walk away with any awards that night, I walked away with something even bigger.

That night, a star was born and I found my purpose.

From that moment on, I knew music wasn't just a side hobby - it was a part of me. Muziki Kama Hii became more than just a song; it became a declaration.

It captured the chaos of searching for my path, the doubt, the uncertainty and then, finally, the magic of finding my rhythm and stepping into my purpose. The rewritten version fully embodies my journey: from questioning my place in the world to standing tall and owning my space.

When you listen to Muziki Kama Hii, I hope you feel that energy. The raw, unfiltered passion. The joy of discovering something that sets your soul on fire.

Whether you're an artist, a dreamer or simply someone trying to figure it all out, this one is for you.

Keep pushing. Keep vibing. Find your purpose.

ENTRY 0120 FEBRUARY 2025SONG DIARY

LEARNING NOT TO HARVEST LOVE FROM THORNS

Where The
Love Is

Fresh heartbreaks and yet another exquisite art.

Family and friends are merely titles if the love isn't real. But I guess we all know this, since some of us find a better brother in a friend than we do in our actual brothers. Maybe that's just how life works, or maybe love isn't always found where we expect it to be.

I'm a problem for real. I might just say how I feel. Curse my bleeding heart!

For days upon days, I searched for roses in places where only thorns existed. Big mistake. Big, huge, enormous mistake with a double chin, thunder thighs and love handles.

When love is not fed to you on a silver spoon, you learn to lick it off knives.

When I first wrote “Where The Love Is,” I started with the chorus, which had nothing to do with romantic love. If anything, romantic love was something I was receiving on a grander scale.

It was never about a lover who walked away, but something deeper. Something I had carried within me for as long as four years and some change.

At some point in my life, I had a tiny fallout with my family and for a while it made me feel like an outsider. Not that I was physically distant, but I was emotionally sidelined. Conversations flowed past me, life was lived despite me, and for a while it made me beg for the love that was hung so high above my head.

While busy pretending to be present, I was in a whole new world inside my head. I slowly learned to mask the loneliness and even accepted living in it.

In an attempt to box myself in with the rest, our established differences could not allow us to see eye to eye on most issues. Warmth and belonging, as natural as they should be within a family, were far out of reach. Yet again, I felt like a visitor overstaying their welcome.

So I did what I do best: camouflage into the shadows and hope they mistake me for a wallflower.

In an environment where love ought to be unconditional, fitting in was quite a task. Being real with myself meant accepting that some cracks never truly mend, and the echoes of such distances might resound for a lifetime.

Through this pain, I birthed my art, and through my art, I learned to let go of things I could not change.

Out of that feeling of exclusion, Where The Love Is became the first destination on my travel list. Eventually, I learned how to let go through my lyrics. When I finally did, I decided to use a romantic disguise to make it less painful and nostalgic.

This would not have been possible without the help of a dear friend, AfriKat, who I surprisingly saw eye to eye with on the execution of this masterpiece.

This song will never be a plea. Neither is it a lament. What it is, is a journey.

My journey of realising that love is not something you seek, but something you nurture. Love is not about waiting to be embraced, but learning to embrace yourself first.

Did you ever feel like a stranger in rooms that should feel like home?

Or a foe in a room full of people you call friends?

Did you ever wonder why love could be so effortless for some and yet so exclusive for you?

Your journey is valid, and in my comment section you may find a soul similar to yours. Do not feel so lonely in this space where love is freely sent, from me to you, and to that 19-year-old me who thought climbing dangerous walls was worth it to harvest love from external sources.

Take your baby steps. Love yourself a little more each day. One day, you might find that the love you were searching for was within you all along.
